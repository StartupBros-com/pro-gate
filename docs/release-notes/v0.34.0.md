## Highlights

- Run durations now separate the time before a run held a review slot from the time after, so slot contention no longer hides inside one blended number.
- `pro-gate-stats` reports those two halves as separate percentiles, and counts the runs that timed out waiting rather than dropping them.
- The round budget now tells you how much wall clock a change has consumed, not just how many rounds it has spent.

## Upgrade

Nothing to do. The existing `secs` field is unchanged and older ledger rows keep working — they simply have no split to report, and the new percentiles are computed from the rows that do.

## What this means in practice

Every finished run appends a line to the ledger, and until now that line carried a single duration: the time from the moment pro-gate started to the moment it settled. That number quietly bundled two very different things. A run that waited thirty-five minutes for a busy account and then worked for ten looked identical to one that started immediately and spent forty-five minutes on the review.

That mattered because the blended figure was the only evidence available for the question people actually ask: is the gate slow, or is the account busy? Those have opposite remedies. Contention is a capacity problem; a slow review is a model-latency problem, and no amount of added concurrency fixes it.

A run now records both halves, split at the moment it acquires its slot. The two always sum to `secs` exactly — the second half is derived from the first rather than measured separately, so even a clock adjustment mid-run cannot make the parts disagree with the whole.

The fields are named for what they actually measure: `pre_slot_secs` and `post_slot_secs`. Slot acquisition is not the same instant as the model starting work — browser startup, fetching and filtering the diff, and preparing the prompt all happen after the slot is held, and a run can still stop there without ever reaching the model. Naming these "queue" and "generation" would promise a precision the timestamps do not have. Separating setup, model work, and recovery is later work; this release measures the boundary it can measure honestly.

The percentiles now draw from the right population. Runs that timed out waiting for a slot are included in the pre-slot figures — they carry the longest and most decision-relevant waits, and excluding them made contention look milder than it was. Collection passes, which never queue and only read back a finished review, are excluded from both figures rather than counted as if they were review work.

The round budget gained the same kind of honesty. Each recorded round now carries how long it took, and `--status` and the round-cap refusal report the total alongside the round count. The total names its own scope: it reads "3 rounds; ~1.4h recorded across 2 scored round(s)" rather than implying every charged round contributed. A round that was charged but never produced a review is counted in the first number and cannot appear in the second, and the wording no longer hides that gap.

## Fixed

- Duration percentiles blended waiting with working, so neither could be read on its own.
- The percentile population excluded the runs that timed out waiting and counted collection passes as review work.
- A backward clock adjustment between the timestamps a run samples could write a negative duration into the ledger and into the percentile sort.
- The round-cap refusal paired a count of every charged round with hours drawn only from rounds that recorded a result, without naming the difference.
